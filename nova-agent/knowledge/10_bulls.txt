# The Ten Bulls of Zen (Ten Oxherding Pictures)

The Ten Bulls is a series of short poems and accompanying drawings used in the Zen Buddhist tradition to describe the stages of a practitioner's progress toward enlightenment, and their subsequent return to society to enact true wisdom.

The "Bull" (or Ox) represents the Mind, or the True Self.

## 1. In Search of the Bull (The Search)
Aimless searching. The practitioner feels lost, separated from their true nature, and desires to find it. The world feels confusing and vast.

## 2. Discovery of the Footprints
By studying the teachings (sutras) and listening to wise guidance, the practitioner discovers the traces of the truth. They haven't seen the Bull yet, but they know it exists and which direction to walk.

## 3. Perceiving the Bull
A first glimpse. The practitioner sees the tail or the rear of the Bull. This is the initial flash of enlightenment (Satori). The senses are heightened, and the connection is felt, though fleeting.

## 4. Catching the Bull
A fierce struggle. The practitioner has caught hold of the mind, but the mind is wild, unruly, and used to its old habits. It requires immense discipline and a tight grip on the "whip and rope" to keep the Bull from running back into the mental mist.

## 5. Taming the Bull
The mind becomes docile. The practitioner no longer needs to exert extreme force. The Bull willingly follows the practitioner. Steady, continuous practice guarantees that the mind remains tranquil and focused.

## 6. Riding the Bull Home
The struggle is over. The practitioner rides leisurely on the Bull's back, playing a flute. There is an effortless state of flow. The practitioner is returning to the true source, the original home of peace.

## 7. The Bull Transcended
The practitioner reaches home. The Bull is forgotten (transcended); the practitioner sits alone in solitary serenity. The idea of "having a mind to tame" has vanished. There is only the person, peaceful and still.

## 8. Both Bull and Self Transcended
Complete emptiness. Represented usually by an empty circle (Enso). All concepts—the self, the mind, enlightenment, the struggle—have disappeared. The ultimate zero-state of the Void.

## 9. Reaching the Source
The practitioner returns to the origin and observes the world purely. Mountains are mountains, rivers are rivers. There is no artificial mental projection onto nature. Things are exactly as they are in their absolute perfection.

## 10. In the World
Returning to society. The enlightened master goes to the marketplace, bare-chested and smiling, carrying a sack of goods. They mingle with ordinary people, drunks, and merchants. Through simple, compassionate, ordinary action, they bring others to enlightenment.
